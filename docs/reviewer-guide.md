# Reviewer Guide

Agent Theories uses structured review states and confidence scoring to distinguish speculation, emerging ideas, partially supported models, and strongly evidenced theories.

## Review goals

Reviewers should evaluate:

- source quality
- evidence quality
- reproducibility
- operational usefulness
- clarity
- licensing safety
- disagreement handling
- uncertainty representation

## Preferred sources

Preferred sources include:

- peer-reviewed literature
- standards bodies
- official technical documentation
- government research institutions
- university publications
- primary-source historical material
- recognized domain experts

## Avoid weak sourcing

Avoid relying primarily on:

- Wikipedia
- SEO blogs
- marketing pages
- social media summaries
- AI-generated text without verification
- unsourced claims

## Confidence scoring guidance

Confidence scores are not truth scores. They estimate confidence in the usefulness and evidence quality of the theory.

| Range | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 0.0 - 0.2 | speculative or weakly supported |
| 0.2 - 0.4 | early-stage or weak evidence |
| 0.4 - 0.6 | partially supported |
| 0.6 - 0.8 | strongly supported in limited contexts |
| 0.8 - 1.0 | broadly validated or operationally proven |

## Review statuses

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| draft | early unpublished or incomplete state |
| submitted | proposed for review |
| reviewed | checked for structure and sources |
| expert-reviewed | reviewed by domain-aware contributors |
| needs-revision | substantial issues identified |

## Important principle

Agent Theories is not trying to enforce a single worldview. Contradictory theories may coexist if uncertainty, criticism, assumptions, and evidence are clearly represented.
